IELTS TASK 1 STRATEGY: PIE CHARTS
--------------------------------------------------

1. CORE STRATEGY & WHAT TO COMPARE:
Pie charts show proportions (percentages) that make up a whole (100%). Usually, you will get two or three pie charts showing different years.

What you MUST compare:
- The largest and smallest slices of the pie.
- Major changes in proportion: Which slice expanded the most? Which slice shrank the most?
- Rank changes: Did a category go from being the smallest slice in 1980 to the biggest slice in 2010?

How to group your data (Body Paragraphs):
- Group by Growth vs. Decline: Body Paragraph 1 describes the slices that grew larger. Body Paragraph 2 describes the slices that became smaller or stayed the same.

--------------------------------------------------
2. BAND 9 EXAMPLE ESSAY

PROMPT: The pie charts compare the primary reasons why students chose to study at a particular UK university in 1980 and 2010.

ESSAY:
The two pie charts illustrate the shifts in the main factors influencing students' decisions to attend a specific university in the United Kingdom, comparing data from the years 1980 and 2010.

Overall, it is evident that the availability of degree courses and the quality of resources became significantly more important to students over the 30-year period. Conversely, the university's proximity to home, which was the dominant factor in 1980, saw a dramatic decline in importance.

In 1980, the location of the university was the primary driving factor, with 35% of students choosing the institution because it was close to their parents' home. However, by 2010, this figure had plummeted to just 10%. Similarly, the appeal of the university's sports and social facilities also waned, dropping by half from 14% in 1980 to a mere 7% three decades later.

In contrast, academic factors gained considerable traction. The proportion of students selecting the university based on the degree courses offered increased from 35% in 1980 to a dominant 45% in 2010. Even more strikingly, the quality of the university's resources, which accounted for only 16% of decisions in 1980, surged to become the second most cited reason in 2010, representing 38% of student responses. (205 words)
