IELTS TASK 1 STRATEGY: PIE CHARTS
--------------------------------------------------
1. CORE STRATEGY & WHAT TO COMPARE:
* Identify the largest and smallest slices in each chart.
* If there are multiple years, focus on the biggest changes (which slices expanded the most, and which shrank the most).
* Pay attention to rank changes (e.g., a slice going from 1st place to 3rd place).

--------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE 1: PAST VS PRESENT (Two Charts)
(Focus: Describing growth and decline over a long period)

CHART DATA (Reasons for choosing a UK university in 1980 and 2010):
1980: Proximity to home (35%) | Degree courses (35%) | Resources (16%) | Sports/Social (14%)
2010: Degree courses (45%) | Resources (38%) | Proximity to home (10%) | Sports/Social (7%)

PROMPT: The pie charts compare the primary reasons why students chose to study at a particular UK university in 1980 and 2010.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (reasons for choosing university, UK, 1980 vs 2010).
* Overview: Highlight the main shifts. Academic reasons (courses/resources) grew in importance, while non-academic reasons (location/sports) declined.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group the declining factors. Detail the massive drop in 'Proximity to home' from 35% to 10%, and the halving of 'Sports/Social'.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group the growing factors. Discuss the rise of 'Degree courses' to 1st place, and the huge surge of 'Resources' from 16% to 38%.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The two pie charts illustrate the shifts in the main factors influencing students' decisions to attend a specific university in the United Kingdom, comparing data from the years 1980 and 2010.

Overall, it is evident that the availability of degree courses and the quality of resources became significantly more important to students over the 30-year period. Conversely, the university's proximity to home, which was a dominant factor in 1980, saw a dramatic decline in importance.

In 1980, the location of the university was tied as the primary driving factor, with 35% of students choosing the institution because it was close to their parents' home. However, by 2010, this figure had plummeted to just 10%. Similarly, the appeal of the university's sports and social facilities also waned, dropping by half from 14% in 1980 to a mere 7% three decades later.

In contrast, academic factors gained considerable traction. The proportion of students selecting the university based on the degree courses offered increased from 35% in 1980 to a dominant 45% in 2010. Even more strikingly, the quality of the university's resources, which accounted for only 16% of decisions in 1980, surged to become the second most cited reason in 2010, representing 38% of student responses. (207 words)

EXAMPLE 2: THREE CHARTS (Comparing different countries)
(Focus: Comparing proportions across geographical regions)

CHART DATA (Water usage in three regions):
North America: Agriculture (39%) | Industrial (48%) | Domestic (13%)
South America: Agriculture (71%) | Industrial (10%) | Domestic (19%)
Europe:        Agriculture (32%) | Industrial (53%) | Domestic (15%)

PROMPT: The pie charts show the proportion of water used for agricultural, industrial, and domestic purposes in North America, South America, and Europe.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (water usage, three sectors, three global regions).
* Overview: Contrast South America (dominated by agriculture) against North America and Europe (dominated by industry). Note domestic is lowest everywhere.
* Body Paragraph 1: Analyze South America in isolation because it's the outlier. Detail its massive 71% agricultural use and tiny 10% industrial use.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group North America and Europe together. Compare their similar patterns, highlighting their high industrial use (48% and 53%) and lower agricultural use.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided pie charts compare the percentage of total water consumption allocated to three different sectors (agriculture, industry, and domestic use) across three major regions: North America, South America, and Europe.

Overall, there is a stark contrast between South America and the other two regions. While South America utilizes the vast majority of its water for agriculture, both North America and Europe dedicate the largest proportion of their water resources to industrial purposes. Domestic water use remains the smallest sector across all three regions.

Looking first at South America, agricultural activities dominate water consumption, accounting for a massive 71% of the total. In stark contrast, industrial usage makes up a mere 10%, while domestic consumption represents 19%, which is notably the highest domestic usage rate among the three regions.

Conversely, the usage patterns in North America and Europe are remarkably similar to one another. In Europe, industrial use is the primary consumer of water at 53%, closely followed by North America at 48%. Agriculture is the second most significant sector in these regions, utilizing 39% of the water in North America and 32% in Europe. Finally, domestic usage accounts for the smallest fraction of water in both of these industrialized regions, at 13% and 15% respectively. (204 words)

EXAMPLE 3: COMBINED WITH TOTAL FIGURES
(Focus: Describing both percentage shifts and total volume increases)

CHART DATA (Global online retail sales and market share):
2015 Total: $1.5 Trillion -> Clothing (40%) | Electronics (35%) | Groceries (25%)
2020 Total: $4.0 Trillion -> Electronics (45%) | Groceries (35%) | Clothing (20%)

PROMPT: The pie charts show the market share of three major retail sectors globally in 2015 and 2020. The total value of all online retail sales is also provided.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (market share, 3 retail sectors, global, 2015 vs 2020, plus total sales value).
* Overview: Mention the massive overall market growth. For sectors, note that Electronics and Groceries grew while Clothing collapsed.
* Body Paragraph 1: Discuss the total market value first. Detail the huge jump from $1.5 trillion to $4.0 trillion.
* Body Paragraph 2: Discuss the shifting percentages. Show how Electronics became the leader, Groceries grew, and Clothing fell from 1st to last place (40% down to 20%).

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The pie charts detail the shifting market share of three primary e-commerce sectors (clothing, electronics, and groceries) on a global scale, comparing the years 2015 and 2020. Additionally, the data provides the total value of all online retail sales for both years.

Overall, the total value of the global online retail market experienced explosive growth over the five-year period. In terms of market share, electronics and groceries expanded their dominance, while the proportion of sales attributed to clothing was halved.

Looking first at the total market value, the online retail sector saw a massive expansion. In 2015, total global sales stood at $1.5 trillion. By 2020, this figure had nearly tripled, reaching a staggering $4.0 trillion. 

Regarding the specific sectors, electronics became the dominant force in the market. Its share grew from 35% in 2015 to nearly half the market (45%) in 2020. Similarly, the proportion of sales generated by groceries saw a significant increase, rising from a quarter of the market (25%) in 2015 to 35% five years later. In stark contrast, clothing, which was the largest sector in 2015 at 40%, suffered a dramatic loss in market share, plummeting to just 20% by 2020, making it the smallest of the three categories. (205 words)

EXAMPLE 4: FOUR CHARTS MATRIX
(Focus: Comparing two locations across two different years simultaneously)

CHART DATA (Energy Production in Country X and Country Y, 1990 vs 2020):
Country X 1990: Fossil Fuels (80%) | Nuclear (15%) | Solar/Wind (5%)
Country X 2020: Fossil Fuels (40%) | Nuclear (20%) | Solar/Wind (40%)
Country Y 1990: Fossil Fuels (90%) | Nuclear (10%) | Solar/Wind (0%)
Country Y 2020: Fossil Fuels (85%) | Nuclear (10%) | Solar/Wind (5%)

PROMPT: The four pie charts compare the sources of energy production in Country X and Country Y in the years 1990 and 2020.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (energy sources, Country X & Y, 1990 vs 2020).
* Overview: Contrast the two nations. Country X modernized heavily towards renewables, while Country Y remained stagnant and reliant on fossil fuels.
* Body Paragraph 1: Focus entirely on Country X. Detail its massive shift from 80% fossil fuels down to 40%, and the equal rise of solar/wind to 40%.
* Body Paragraph 2: Focus entirely on Country Y. Detail its stubborn reliance on fossil fuels (90% to 85%), with renewables barely registering at 5%.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The four pie charts detail the percentage of energy produced from three different sources—fossil fuels, nuclear power, and solar/wind energy—in Country X and Country Y, comparing data from 1990 and 2020.

Overall, while both countries remained reliant on fossil fuels to some degree, Country X underwent a massive transition towards renewable energy over the thirty-year period. In contrast, Country Y's energy infrastructure remained largely unchanged, heavily dominated by fossil fuels.

Analyzing Country X, a dramatic shift in energy policy is evident. In 1990, fossil fuels were the overwhelming primary source, accounting for 80% of total production, while solar and wind made up a negligible 5%. However, by 2020, reliance on fossil fuels had been halved to 40%. This decline was entirely offset by a massive surge in solar and wind energy, which skyrocketed to equal fossil fuels at 40%. Nuclear energy also saw a slight increase from 15% to 20%.

Conversely, Country Y experienced very little modernization. In 1990, a staggering 90% of its energy came from fossil fuels, with nuclear power providing the remaining 10%. Over the next three decades, the situation barely shifted. By 2020, fossil fuel reliance had only dropped by a marginal 5%, allowing for a tiny 5% introduction of solar and wind energy, while nuclear production remained completely stagnant at 10%. (219 words)

EXAMPLE 5: AGE DEMOGRAPHICS
(Focus: Describing age ranges and vocabulary for populations)

CHART DATA (Population of City Z by Age Group, 2022):
Under 18 (25%) | 18-35 (40%) | 36-55 (20%) | Over 55 (15%)

PROMPT: The pie chart shows the age distribution of the population in City Z in the year 2022.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (age distribution, City Z, 2022).
* Overview: State the main finding: it's a very young city. The vast majority are under 36, and the elderly make up the smallest fraction.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group the youth (Under 18 and 18-35). Use fractions: 'two-fifths' for 40% and 'a quarter' for 25%. Sum them up to show they are nearly two-thirds combined.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group the older residents (36-55 and Over 55). Use fractions: 'one-fifth' for 20% and note the 15% elderly group is the smallest slice.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided pie chart breaks down the demographic composition of City Z in the year 2022, categorizing the population into four distinct age brackets.

Overall, it is immediately apparent that City Z possesses a relatively young population. The vast majority of its residents are under the age of 36, while senior citizens make up the smallest proportion of the community.

Looking closely at the younger demographics, the 18 to 35 age group constitutes the largest single segment of the population, accounting for exactly two-fifths (40%) of the total residents. When combined with the 'Under 18' category, which represents a quarter of the population (25%), it is clear that nearly two-thirds of the entire city is aged 35 or younger.

In contrast, older residents form a much smaller minority. Individuals falling into the 36 to 55 age bracket make up one-fifth (20%) of the city's demographic makeup. Finally, the oldest category, consisting of individuals aged over 55, represents the smallest slice of the pie chart, accounting for a mere 15% of the total population in City Z. (183 words)
