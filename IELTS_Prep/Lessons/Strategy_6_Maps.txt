IELTS TASK 1 STRATEGY: MAPS
--------------------------------------------------
1. CORE STRATEGY & WHAT TO COMPARE:
* Maps test your vocabulary of LOCATION (north, south, adjacent to, opposite) and CHANGE (demolished, constructed, expanded, replaced).
* Look for what was added, what was removed, and what stayed the same.
* Group by geography (e.g., Body 1 = the north side of town, Body 2 = the south side) OR by category (Body 1 = housing, Body 2 = infrastructure/roads).

--------------------------------------------------
EXAMPLE 1: PAST VS PRESENT (Town Development)
(Focus: Passive voice in the past tense - e.g., "was demolished", "were constructed")

MAP DATA (Village of Ryden, 1990 vs 2010):
1990: West (Farmland), East (Small housing estate), Center (Post Office & small shop), North (Forest)
2010: West (Golf course), East (Large housing estate - expanded), Center (Post Office remains, shop replaced by Supermarket), North (Forest cut down, replaced by a school)

PROMPT: The maps below show the village of Ryden in 1990 and 2010. Summarise the information.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (maps, village of Ryden, 1990 vs 2010).
* Overview: State the main transformation: Ryden changed from rural/agricultural to residential/developed.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group by Geography (West and North). Detail the loss of farmland to a golf course, and the forest being replaced by a school.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group by Geography (East and Center). Detail the expansion of the housing estate, the surviving post office, and the shop becoming a supermarket.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The two maps illustrate the extensive development and modernization that took place in the village of Ryden over a twenty-year period, comparing its layout in 1990 to that in 2010.

Overall, it is immediately apparent that Ryden transformed from a rural, agricultural village into a significantly more residential and developed area. The most notable changes include the loss of farmland and woodland, which were replaced by recreational facilities and educational infrastructure.

Looking first at the western and northern sections of the village, major environmental changes occurred. In 1990, the western side was dominated by agricultural farmland; however, by 2010, this entire area had been converted into a large golf course. Similarly, the forest that previously stood in the north was completely cut down, and a new school was constructed in its place.

Turning to the eastern and central areas, the village became far more commercialized and populated. The small housing estate located in the east in 1990 was significantly expanded by 2010 to accommodate more residents. In the center of the village, while the post office remained in its original location, the small shop adjacent to it was demolished and replaced by a much larger supermarket. (197 words)

EXAMPLE 2: PRESENT VS FUTURE (Proposals)
(Focus: Future passive grammar - e.g., "is planned to be built", "will be relocated")

MAP DATA (University Campus, Present vs Proposed Future Layout):
Present: North (Car park), Center (Library), South (Science block & Arts block), East (Open green space)
Future: North (Car park expanded), Center (Library demolished, new Student Union built), South (Arts block remains, Science block expanded), East (Green space replaced by new Library)

PROMPT: The maps show the current layout of a university campus and the proposed changes for the future. Summarise the information.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (current layout, proposed future changes, university campus).
* Overview: Note the massive redevelopment focusing on student/academic facilities, specifically sacrificing green space to move the library.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group the North and East. Use future passive tense to describe expanding the car park and building the new library over the green space.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group the Center and South. Detail the demolition of the old library for a Student Union, the static Arts block, and the expanded Science block.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided maps depict the current infrastructure of a university campus and outline the proposed developments planned for its future layout.

Overall, the campus is slated to undergo significant redevelopment, prioritizing the expansion of academic and student facilities. This will primarily involve the relocation of the library and the sacrifice of existing green spaces.

Analyzing the northern and eastern sections of the campus, major construction is proposed. The existing car park located in the north is planned to be substantially expanded to accommodate more vehicles. Meanwhile, the large, open green space currently situated on the eastern side of the campus will be completely built over. According to the plans, a brand-new, larger library will be constructed on this site.

Regarding the central and southern areas, further modernizations are scheduled. The original library, which currently sits in the center of the campus, will be demolished. In its place, a new Student Union building will be erected. Looking to the south, the Arts block is the only structure that will remain entirely unchanged. However, the Science block situated next to it will undergo an extension, significantly increasing its footprint on the campus. (192 words)

EXAMPLE 3: SITE SELECTION (Comparing two locations)
(Focus: Comparing advantages and geographical placement)

MAP DATA (Proposed sites for a new shopping mall in the town of Garlsdon):
Site 1 (S1): North of town, surrounded by countryside, near a main road (A4) and a railway line.
Site 2 (S2): Center of town, surrounded by housing, no main roads, pedestrian access only.

PROMPT: The map below shows two possible sites (S1 and S2) for a new shopping mall in the town of Garlsdon. Summarise the information.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (two proposed sites, S1 and S2, shopping mall in Garlsdon).
* Overview: State the core contrast: S1 has great vehicular transport links, while S2 is central and relies on pedestrian access.
* Body Paragraph 1: Analyze Site 1 exclusively. Detail its northern countryside location and its excellent proximity to the A4 road and railway.
* Body Paragraph 2: Analyze Site 2 exclusively. Detail its central location surrounded by housing, and point out its major flaw (no roads/trains nearby).

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided map displays the town of Garlsdon and highlights two potential locations, designated as S1 and S2, that are currently being considered for the construction of a new shopping mall.

Overall, the two sites offer vastly different geographical advantages. While S1 is situated on the outskirts of the town with excellent transport links, S2 is located directly in the town center, offering closer proximity to residential areas but limited vehicular access.

Looking first at Site 1, it is located in the northern countryside, entirely outside the main residential zones of Garlsdon. However, its primary advantage lies in its accessibility for commuters. The site is situated immediately adjacent to a major arterial road, the A4, and is also positioned alongside the town’s railway line. This would make it highly accessible for shoppers traveling from outside the immediate area via car or train.

In contrast, Site 2 proposes a completely different retail environment. It is located squarely in the center of Garlsdon, completely surrounded by housing estates, making it easily accessible on foot for local residents. However, unlike S1, there are no major transport links nearby. The area is entirely pedestrianized, meaning the site cannot be accessed directly by road or rail. (201 words)

EXAMPLE 4: FLOOR PLAN MODIFICATION
(Focus: Interior layout changes and specific directional vocabulary)

MAP DATA (Central Library Ground Floor, 2001 vs Present):
2001: Entrance (South), Children's Books (North-East), Adult Fiction (North-West), Reading Room (South-East).
Present: Entrance (South), Children's Books (Moved to South-East, replacing Reading Room), Adult Fiction (Expanded across the entire North), IT Center (New - built in the North-East).

PROMPT: The plans below show the layout of the ground floor of a public library in 2001 and how it looks today. Summarise the information.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (layout changes, ground floor, public library, 2001 vs today).
* Overview: Note the shift towards technology (IT center) and the massive expansion of adult fiction at the cost of the reading room.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group the Northern half. Detail the massive expansion of Adult Fiction across the whole north wall, and the new IT Center replacing the old children's section.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group the Southern half. Note the static entrance, the dismantling of the Reading Room, and the relocation of Children's Books to that spot.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided floor plans illustrate the structural modifications and reorganization of the ground floor of a public library, comparing its layout in 2001 with its present-day configuration.

Overall, the library has undergone significant interior modernization, transitioning from a traditional reading-focused layout to a more technologically integrated space. Notably, the area dedicated to adult fiction has been vastly expanded, while the traditional reading room has been entirely eliminated.

Looking at the northern section of the building, the most dramatic expansion has occurred. In 2001, the Adult Fiction section occupied only the north-western corner, while the north-eastern corner housed the Children’s Books. Currently, the Adult Fiction section has been extended to span the entire northern wall of the library. Furthermore, a completely new IT Center has been constructed, replacing the former children's area in the north-east.

Turning to the southern half of the floor plan, the main entrance has remained in its original location on the southern wall. However, the space to the right of the entrance (the south-east corner), which previously served as a dedicated Reading Room, has been repurposed. The Reading Room has been dismantled, and the Children’s Books section has been relocated to this area. (202 words)

EXAMPLE 5: ISLAND RESORT DEVELOPMENT
(Focus: Describing natural landscapes transforming into commercial zones)

MAP DATA (An Island, Before and After Tourist Development):
Before: Empty island, beach on west side, trees scattered, no infrastructure.
After: Pier added to South, Reception built in center. Restaurant built in North. Accommodation (huts) built around the beach and in the east. Footpaths connect margins.

PROMPT: The two maps below show an island before and after the construction of some tourist facilities. Summarise the information.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (island, before and after construction, tourist facilities).
* Overview: State the obvious: it went from a completely untouched natural landscape to a heavily commercialized resort.
* Body Paragraph 1: Focus purely on the 'Before' map. Describe the empty island, the beach on the west, the scattered trees, and the lack of access.
* Body Paragraph 2: Focus purely on the 'After' map. Detail the new pier, the central reception, the northern restaurant, and the two zones of accommodation huts connected by paths.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The two maps depict a small island and illustrate the extensive construction work that has transformed it from a completely uninhabited natural landscape into a developed tourist resort.

Overall, it is clear that the island has been heavily commercialized. The previously untouched environment has been equipped with comprehensive tourist infrastructure, including accommodation, dining facilities, and a dedicated transport link, while retaining its natural beach.

Prior to development, the island was entirely devoid of any man-made structures. It consisted only of a large beach on its western coast and several clusters of trees scattered across the central and eastern areas. There was no way to access the island by boat.

Following the construction phase, a pier was built on the southern coast, allowing boats to dock. From this pier, a vehicle track leads directly to a newly constructed central Reception building. The island now features two distinct accommodation zones consisting of small huts connected by footpaths: one group is situated around the western beach, and the other is located in the east. Finally, a large restaurant has been erected in the northern part of the island, accessible via a footpath from the central reception area. (199 words)

EXAMPLE 6: MUSEUM LAYOUT CHANGES
(Focus: Prepositions of place inside a building)

MAP DATA (City Museum, 1990 vs 2010):
1990: Entrance (South). Left of entrance: Gift Shop. Right of entrance: Cloakroom. Center: Local History. Back (North): Dinosaur Exhibition.
2010: Entrance (South). Left: Cafe (replaced Gift Shop). Right: Gift Shop & Cloakroom (shared space). Center: Local History (expanded). Back: Dinosaur Exh (removed), replaced by Interactive Tech Zone.

PROMPT: The plans show the changes made to the interior layout of a city museum between 1990 and 2010.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (interior modifications, city museum, 1990 vs 2010).
* Overview: Note the major restructuring: adding catering (cafe) and replacing the old dinosaur exhibit with modern technology.
* Body Paragraph 1: Group the Northern/Back half. Detail the removal of the dinosaurs, the new Tech Zone, and the expansion of Local History in the center.
* Body Paragraph 2: Group the Southern/Entrance area. Describe the gift shop converting to a cafe, and the gift shop moving to share space with the cloakroom.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The provided floor plans illustrate the internal modifications and the reallocation of space within a city museum, comparing its layout in the years 1990 and 2010.

Overall, the museum underwent significant restructuring to modernize its facilities and expand its core exhibitions. The most prominent change was the complete removal of the dinosaur exhibition to make way for a new technological zone, alongside the introduction of catering facilities.

Looking first at the northern half of the building, a major shift in focus occurred. In 1990, the entire back section of the museum was dedicated to a Dinosaur Exhibition. However, by 2010, this exhibition had been completely removed. In its place, a modern Interactive Tech Zone was constructed. Immediately south of this, the Local History section, which occupied the center of the building in 1990, was significantly expanded in 2010, taking up a much larger central footprint.

Turning to the southern entrance area, visitor amenities were entirely reorganized. In 1990, a gift shop was located to the left of the main entrance, while a cloakroom sat to the right. By 2010, the original gift shop was converted into a cafe. Consequently, the gift shop was relocated to the right side of the entrance, where it now shares a condensed space with the existing cloakroom. (215 words)

EXAMPLE 7: ROAD NETWORK CHANGES
(Focus: Infrastructure vocabulary like 'roundabout', 'intersection', and 'pedestrianized')

MAP DATA (City Centre Roads, 2005 vs Present):
2005: Crossroads intersection in center. North street (two-way). South street (two-way). East street (two-way). West street (two-way).
Present: Crossroads replaced by a large Roundabout. North street (Pedestrianized - no cars). South street (widened to 4 lanes). East/West (remain two-way). New bridge built over the East street.

PROMPT: The maps illustrate the changes that have taken place in the road network of a city centre from 2005 to the present day.

PLANNING NOTE:
* Intro: Paraphrase the prompt (road network modifications, city centre, 2005 vs present).
* Overview: Highlight the goal of improving traffic flow (roundabout) and prioritizing pedestrian safety (pedestrianization).
* Body Paragraph 1: Focus purely on the Central intersection. Describe the demolition of the crossroads and the construction of the large roundabout.
* Body Paragraph 2: Focus on the individual streets branching off. Detail the pedestrianized North street, the widened 4-lane South street, and the new bridge over the East street.

BAND 9 ESSAY:
The two maps depict the structural developments and traffic management alterations implemented within a city centre's road network, comparing its layout in 2005 with its current design.

Overall, the road system has been heavily modified to improve traffic flow and prioritize pedestrian safety in certain areas. The most significant changes include the replacement of the central intersection with a roundabout and the pedestrianization of the northern route.

Looking at the center of the network, the original 2005 layout featured a standard crossroads intersection where all four directional streets met. Presently, this crossroads has been entirely demolished and replaced by a large, central roundabout connecting the remaining active roads. 

Regarding the specific arterial routes, dramatic changes were made to the north-south axis. The northern street, which was a standard two-way road in 2005, has now been completely pedestrianized, barring vehicle access entirely. To compensate, the southern street has been significantly widened, expanding from a standard two-way road into a four-lane highway. Finally, while the eastern and western streets remain unchanged as two-way roads, a new bridge has been constructed directly over the eastern route, likely to facilitate uninterrupted pedestrian crossing. (193 words)
